How fast is WA's economy growing right now?

Official figures on spending in Western Australia arrive about two months after each quarter ends. This model reads the indicators that come out sooner, such as jobs, wages, house approvals and the iron ore price, and estimates the answer early.

How it works

What is being estimated

Growth in WA State Final Demand, the Australian Bureau of Statistics' quarterly measure of spending by households, businesses and government in the state, with price changes and seasonal patterns removed. Gross State Product is only published once a year, so this is the closest quarterly read on the WA economy. It leaves out exports, which matter a great deal in a state built on iron ore and gas, so the iron ore and oil prices are included as inputs instead.

The models

Three machine learning models are trained on every quarter since the early 1990s. Ridge regression is a linear model that shrinks weak signals towards zero. A random forest averages hundreds of decision trees. Gradient boosting builds small trees one after another, each correcting the errors of the last. A blend of all three is also tried. Two simple benchmarks sit alongside them, the average of the last two years and a model that only knows the last two quarters of growth.

Construction work done and business investment for the quarter come out a few days before the official demand figure. Once they do, a second version of each model that also uses them takes over for those final days.

Monthly indicators are read over the latest three months against the three months before, so a quarter with only some of its figures in is still compared like for like. Perth inflation and wage growth come in a quarter behind, because the figures for the quarter being estimated are published too late to use.

Each model is tested by stepping through history from 2010, predicting each quarter using only the quarters before it and only the figures that would have been published by then. Whichever has the smallest typical miss produces the headline figure, and the likely range comes from its own past misses at the same point in the quarter, covering eight in every ten outcomes.

Things to keep in mind

The test uses today's version of the data. Official figures get revised, so at the time the model would have seen slightly different numbers, and its real accuracy is probably a little worse than shown. The two pandemic quarters of 2020 are left out of training and scoring because nothing in normal history looks like them. Early in a quarter the estimate rests on fewer figures, and it is refreshed each month as more come in.

Sources

Australian Bureau of Statistics Data API for State Final Demand, the Labour Force survey, the monthly and quarterly Consumer Price Index, the Wage Price Index, Building Approvals, Construction Work Done and Private New Capital Expenditure. Iron ore, Brent oil and the exchange rate come from the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (FRED), originally from the IMF and the US Federal Reserve. The model refreshes on the 20th of each month, and again late in February, May, August and November once the construction and business investment surveys are out.
