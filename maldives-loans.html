Who lends to Maldives, and for what?

Every active external loan to the government of Maldives, mapped by the type of lender and the Sustainable Development Goal its project mainly serves.

I built this out of curiosity, and to learn how an SDG financing map is put together. It starts from the Ministry of Finance register of active external borrowings at 30 June 2026. Each lender is grouped as multilateral, bilateral, export credit or commercial, and each loan is matched to a goal from its project name. Amounts are what was committed when each loan was signed, converted to US dollars at 30 June 2026 rates. They are not what is owed today.

For the grants, see Who gives Maldives grants, and for what?

Which goals do the loans serve?

Each loan is counted once, under the goal its project name points to most clearly. Many projects serve more than one goal, and the second goal is shown in the table below but not added here, so nothing is counted twice. Select a goal to list its loans.

How has the mix of lenders changed?

Each column holds the loans signed in that period which are still active. Loans that have been fully repaid are no longer in the register, so earlier periods show only the longest running loans, and their totals are smaller than what was borrowed at the time.

Who funds which goals?

Each row is a lender and each column a goal. The darker the square, the more that lender has committed to that goal, in millions of US dollars. Switch to share to see how each lender divides its own lending. Select a square to list those loans.

Loans the government guarantees

State-owned companies, and one private company, also borrow with a guarantee from the government, which promises to repay if the borrower cannot. These loans are listed in a separate Ministry of Finance register of active sovereign guarantees, so they are shown here on their own and not added to the totals above. Amounts are the sums guaranteed when each loan was signed, converted at 30 June 2026 rates, not what is still owed.

How complete is this?

The register is the official list of the government's own external loans. Some public borrowing sits outside it.

How this was made

Source and date

The Ministry of Finance register of active external borrowings of the government of Maldives, as at 30 June 2026. It lists each loan's signing date, lender, project, currency, committed amount and implementation status.

Converting currencies

The register gives each amount in the currency the loan was signed in. Every amount is converted to US dollars at a single date, 30 June 2026, using the rates below. The same rate is used for a loan signed in 1985 as for one signed in 2025, so the figures show what the commitments are worth at today's rates, not their value when signed.

How lenders were grouped
  • Multilateral. Development banks and funds owned by several countries, such as the World Bank's IDA, the Asian Development Bank, AIIB, the Islamic Development Bank, the OPEC Fund, the European Investment Bank, IFAD and the Nordic Development Fund.
  • Bilateral. Lending from one government or its development fund, such as the Saudi Fund for Development, the Kuwait Fund, the Abu Dhabi Fund for Development and the Government of India.
  • Export credit. Export credit agencies and buyers' credit, such as the Export-Import Banks of India and China and Hungarian Exim. These loans are usually tied to goods or contractors from the lending country.
  • Commercial. Banks and bondholders lending on market terms.

The grouping is kept in an editable lookup file, so any lender can be moved and the page rebuilt.

How goals were assigned

Each project name is checked against a list of keywords. Water and sewerage point to SDG 6, hospitals to SDG 3, schools to SDG 4, power and renewables to SDG 7, airports, roads, harbours and ports to SDG 9, housing and urban development to SDG 11, coastal protection and climate adaptation to SDG 13 and fisheries to SDG 14. Budget support and balance of payments loans are grouped as general support rather than given a goal.

Each match is given a confidence level. A specific word such as sewerage is high, a broad word such as urban is medium, and a generic phrase such as infrastructure is low. Where a name points to two goals, the clearer one becomes the main goal and the other the second goal. Loans whose names match nothing are left unclassified rather than guessed. The low confidence and unclassified loans were then checked by hand, and those corrections take priority over the keyword rules every time the data is rebuilt. Loans that refinance an earlier loan are tagged to the goal of the project they refinance, with general support as the second goal.

Guaranteed loans

The section on guaranteed loans comes from the Ministry of Finance register of active sovereign guarantees at 30 June 2026, which lists each guarantee to state-owned companies and private entities with its date, lender, borrower, purpose, currency and amount. Amounts are the sums guaranteed when signed, not what is still owed. Rufiyaa amounts are converted at the Maldives Monetary Authority reference rate of 15.42 per US dollar, and other currencies at the rates above. Lenders are grouped through an editable lookup, which adds banks operating in Maldives as their own group. Goals are assigned from the purpose using the same keyword rules as the main register, and working capital, overdraft and trade finance lines are labelled company finance rather than given a goal.

What this does not show
  • Commitments, not money spent or owed. The amounts are what was agreed when each loan was signed. They are not disbursements, and not the debt outstanding today, which is lower for older loans that are partly repaid.
  • Active loans only. Loans that have been fully repaid are not in the register, so earlier periods are understated in the chart of lenders over time.
  • Project names can be vague. A goal is only as good as the project name it was read from. Some names describe several projects, or a credit line used for many.
  • One loan, several goals. A harbour can serve jobs, transport and climate resilience at once. The charts count only the main goal so totals add up.
  • No loan terms. The register does not give interest rates, grace periods or maturities, so the page does not show how concessional each loan is.
  • Loans only, not grants. The register lists borrowing, so grants are not included. Much of the support Maldives receives from UN agencies, Japan, the World Bank and others comes as grants or technical assistance, and none of it appears here. Grants are mapped on a companion page.
  • Only the loan part of mixed financing. Some projects are paid for partly by a loan and partly by a grant, and only the loan appears here. The World Bank's Enhancing Employability and Resilience of Youth project, for example, had US$ 20 million of IDA financing, half as a credit and half as a grant, and the register shows the credit alone. For lenders that mix the two, the totals here are lower than the full value of the projects they support.
  • Outside the register. Debt guaranteed by the government for state owned enterprises is shown in its own section and not added to the totals. Domestic borrowing by the government and private investment are not included.
Files

The tagged dataset is free to download as a CSV file, and the page reads from this JSON file. A Python script cleans the register, converts the currencies, groups the lenders and tags the goals, so the page can be rebuilt whenever a new register is published.
