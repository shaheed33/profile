What adds to final-year funding pressure?

Funding pressure = grant loss + additional wage cost + extra capital renewal. It measures the change from a flat base, not a forecast cash shortfall. Financing, reserves, depreciation changes and council responses are excluded.

Research / Early warning

Can deterioration be spotted earlier?

The question is whether grant dependence, liquidity, renewal spending, population change and past performance predict a weaker operating result two or three years later.

What is built

A council-year panel with explicit unit conversions and missing values. A retrospective pipeline compares a council fixed-effects regression and gradient boosting with a simple “no change” baseline.

Training uses only outcomes dated before each test origin. Each fold fits its own missing-value treatment and scaling. Two- and three-year targets use exact year matches.

What is still needed

A longer, consistent financial history; checked publication dates and council boundaries; and an untouched final test period. ABS population context is already included through the OLG files. A richer demographic and socioeconomic panel remains to be assembled.

Until those checks pass, no council receives a predicted risk score, forecast interval or model-based ranking.

Open methods

Follow the numbers

Source data and missing years

Source: NSW Office of Local Government, Your Council time series, using the 2022–23, 2023–24 and 2024–25 workbooks. Population and five-year population change are ABS figures reproduced by OLG; their reference periods can differ from the financial year.

The annual files change units: 2022–23 financial totals are in thousands of dollars; later totals are dollars. Ratios also switch between percentages and fractions. The build uses reviewed, file-specific conversions. “Not collected” cells are never filled with another year’s value. Some councils have no reported financial data.

Exactly how the scenario works

Let R be base revenue, E expenses, g the share of grants and contributions, c the grant cut, p the assumed payroll share, w annual wage growth, and a extra renewal as a share of base revenue. At year t:

  • Revenue = R − R × g × c.
  • Expenses = E + E × p × [(1 + w)t − 1].
  • Additional capital renewal = R × a each year.
  • Additional funding pressure = grant loss + extra wage cost + extra renewal.

Other income and costs stay flat. Grants include capital and contributions, so the calculation does not isolate recurrent income. Capital renewal does not immediately reduce the revenue-less-expenses balance; later depreciation, interest, financing, timing and asset benefits are not modelled. Do not interpret the result as a statutory budget forecast.

Liquidity, uncertainty and interpretation

Historical cash-cover and current ratios provide context only. Current unrestricted cash, liabilities, payroll and a detailed capital programme are unavailable here, so the tool cannot forecast liquidity or borrowing needs. Its scenarios have no statistical confidence bands: changing assumptions explores exposure, not probability. A forecast model would need independent time-based validation before intervals or risk probabilities could be justified.
